Overview

Drawing on project-level data from 2014 to 2023, the book traces the development of China's PPP program and examines how national policy, local implementation, state-owned enterprise participation, regional diffusion, financing incentives, and fiscal constraints shaped its trajectory.

The analysis brings together spatial diffusion theory, transaction cost theory, capital structure theory, and cutback management theory. The result is an institutional account of both PPP expansion and the governance problems that followed.

Chapter Guide

  1. Introduction

    Introduces core PPP concepts and practices across countries, the theoretical perspectives used in the book, the project-level dataset, and the overall interdisciplinary framework.

  2. The Rise and Fall of China's Public-Private Partnership: A Ten-Year Review (2014–2023)

    Uses descriptive statistics, figures, and tables to examine the institutional background, development patterns, achievements, and limitations of the PPP system.

  3. The Predominance of State-Owned Enterprises in Public-Private Partnership Implementation

    Uses empirical analysis to examine the extensive involvement of state-owned capital as a defining feature of the PPP model.

  4. The Diffusion Mechanism of Public-Private Partnerships among Subnational Governments

    Draws on spatial diffusion theory to investigate the mechanisms behind the rapid expansion of PPPs across regions.

  5. Public-Private Partnership as a Financing Instrument for Subnational Governments

    Examines PPP financing within a capital-structure framework and analyzes it alongside debt financing.

  6. Local Government Financing and the Fiscal Effects on Government Expenditure

    Applies cutback management theory to assess how fiscal stress arising from PPP and debt obligations affects local government expenditure allocation.

  7. Fiscal Rules and the Control of Public-Private Partnership Expansion

    Evaluates whether fiscal rules constrain PPP expansion and examines the conditions and consequences of rule failure.

  8. Conclusions and Policy Implications

    Synthesizes the preceding findings and presents their policy implications.
